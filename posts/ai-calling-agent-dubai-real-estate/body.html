Why do Dubai property leads go cold?

Dubai sales teams work in conditions that make slow follow-up almost inevitable.

  • Many sources. Portals, ads, WhatsApp, referrals and walk-ins each deliver enquiries to a different place.
  • Buyers in other time zones. An investor in London, Mumbai or Toronto enquires when your office is closed.
  • Launch-day spikes. A new off-plan release brings more enquiries in a day than the team can call back.
  • Brokers out at viewings. The people who would make the call are usually with other clients.

Speed often decides who gets the first real conversation. A buyer who has enquired with several brokers tends to keep talking to the ones who answer.

What is an AI calling agent?

An AI calling agent is software that makes and takes phone calls in natural spoken language, answers from information your company has approved, books the next step and hands over to a person with the full context.

It does not replace your brokers. It makes the first call, so a broker's first call is with a buyer whose needs are already written down.

What does the call actually sound like?

A typical call to a new enquiry runs like this.

  1. It calls within minutes, at a sensible hour for the buyer's time zone, not only Dubai's.
  2. It introduces itself as an automated assistant from your brokerage and says why it is calling.
  3. It asks short questions, one at a time: a rough budget range in the buyer's own words, the preferred area, ready or off-plan, the timeline, cash or mortgage, and whether the buyer lives in the UAE or abroad.
  4. It answers simple questions from approved information, such as location, unit types and handover stage.
  5. It offers the next step: a viewing slot, a video walkthrough for an overseas buyer, or a broker callback.
  6. It writes the outcome and a short summary to your CRM.

If nobody answers, it retries at times you agree and sends a message. The lead is then marked for a broker.

Where does the broker take over?

The agent qualifies and books. Your brokers decide and negotiate. A broker takes over for:

  • negotiation and any offer
  • pricing questions, including payment plans, beyond the approved information
  • sensitive cases, such as an upset or confused buyer
  • anyone who asks for a person
  • anything outside the approved information, where the agent says it does not know

The broker receives the buyer's name, area, budget range, timeline, financing and location, plus the outcome of the call. The buyer does not repeat anything. Your team also reads call transcripts regularly. Where the agent struggled, the script or the source information is corrected.

Can it re-engage old enquiries and remind buyers before viewings?

Yes. Both are routine work that brokers rarely have time for.

Before a new launch, the agent can call past enquirers who asked about a matching area or unit type, and who agreed to be contacted. It asks whether they are still looking, shares approved details and offers a viewing or a walkthrough.

Before a viewing, it confirms with the buyer and reschedules if plans have changed. Your brokers then spend their time on viewings that will actually happen.

What happens on WhatsApp after the call?

The call is rarely the last step. After it, the buyer can receive a WhatsApp message with the agreed viewing details and the brochure your team has approved. The same assistant answers follow-up questions on WhatsApp and on your website, using the same approved information and the same record.

WhatsApp has a rule that shapes this. A business can reply freely for 24 hours after the customer's last message. After that, it can send only pre-approved templates. What should you prepare before going live?

A short list gets a first version moving quickly.

  1. List your enquiry sources and where each one lands today.
  2. Write down the questions you ask every new buyer, in the order you ask them.
  3. Gather approved project information: locations, unit types, handover stage and brochures.
  4. Decide what the agent may say about pricing and what always goes to a broker.
  5. Name who receives each handover and the hours they are available.
  6. Agree calling hours and the consent wording on your enquiry forms.
  7. Collect past enquiries and any call recordings you can share.
  8. Note your baseline: time to first call and viewings booked.
