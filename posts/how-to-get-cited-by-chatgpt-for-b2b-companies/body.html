Why does ChatGPT cite some B2B companies and not others?

An assistant that answers with sources does three things. It reads the question, searches the web for pages that match, and then writes an answer from the pages it finds. A company gets named when its page is found, is easy to quote and looks trustworthy.

That gives you three jobs:

  • Be findable. The page must be indexed and ranking for the question. A page that search engines have not indexed cannot be cited.
  • Be quotable. The answer must sit in the first lines, in plain words, with a table or steps where they help.
  • Be believable. Real facts, dated numbers and named examples beat vague claims.

How do you get cited by ChatGPT? A 6-step checklist

  1. List the buyer questions. Write 20 questions a buyer asks before choosing a supplier. Use your sales calls, your Search Console queries and the "People also ask" box on Google.
  2. Answer first. Put the direct answer in the first 40 to 60 words of the page. Then add detail below it.
  3. Use question-style headings. Headings that match how buyers type make it easy for a tool to pull the right section.
  4. Add real facts. Use your own data, dated results and named examples. Generic text gives an assistant no reason to pick you over the next page.
  5. Get the page indexed. Check the page is in your sitemap, and ask Google to index it through Search Console. A page nobody can find cannot be cited.
  6. Link the cluster. Connect each post to related posts and to the service page. A group of linked posts on one topic signals that you know the subject.

How do you know if ChatGPT is mentioning your company?

Search Console cannot show traffic from AI answers. You have to check by hand, and keep the method the same each time.

  1. Fix a list. Pick 10 to 20 buyer questions and keep them unchanged.
  2. Ask in a clean window. Use a fresh, logged-out session in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, so your own history does not shape the answer.
  3. Record the result. Note which companies are named, which pages are cited, and whether you appear.
  4. Find the gaps. Where a competitor is named and you are not, read the page that was cited. Ask what it covers that yours does not.
  5. Repeat weekly. Answers shift. A weekly check shows the trend.

Treat the results as a guide, not a score. Assistants can give different answers to the same question, so look for patterns across several weeks.

What should a human still own?

Automation can run most of this, but three things should stay with your team:

  • The facts. What you sell, to whom, and what you can honestly claim. A good setup writes only from an approved facts sheet.
  • The proof. Client results and screenshots need the client's yes.
  • The buyer. Which customers you want decides which questions you answer.

Do not try to trick an assistant with hidden text, fake reviews or invented statistics. It does not work for long, and it harms the trust you are trying to build.

Who is this right for, and who is it not for?

It fits B2B companies where a single customer is worth a lot, buyers research before they call, and no one on the team owns the blog. Manufacturers, distributors, software firms, consultancies and B2B services are typical.
